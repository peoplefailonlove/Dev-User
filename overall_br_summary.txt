Across 26 BR respondents (mix of Controllers, VPs, AR/AR Managers and AP leads) clear patterns emerge: primary feature demand centers on a daily consolidated remittance file (Remittance File) plus seamless compatibility with existing automation / ERP (Works with your automation & ERP). Closely behind are Enterprise Portal, Automated Delivery (payment preferences), Tailored Remittance and Custom Reports; Check Conversion (checks → ACH/card) is repeatedly called out as the fastest path to reduce DSO. Top prioritized benefits are: (1) reduce manual remittance work and errors, (2) receive a single daily remittance file to speed cash application, (3) save analyst time/costs and reallocate to growth work, (4) faster collections / lower DSO via check conversion, and (5) minimal IT effort & fast, non‑suite implementations. Quantitative signals: average willingness to pay ≈ 2.7 on a 1–4 scale (median ~3), median reported DSO ≈ 45 days (many reports 30–68 days), and paper checks persist—typically ~5–10% of payment mix (many specific calls in the 2–11% range). Automation maturity and familiarity skew moderate: most report Automation ≈ 2 (low–moderate) and decision‑making influence typically DM ≈ 2–3. Behavioral traits and pain points: respondents are pragmatic and integration‑driven (want solutions that “work with” ERPs and cash application tools), value low‑touch deployments and security controls, and are sensitive to customer willingness to adopt portals. Common friction includes missing remittances/unclear payment data, heavy existing investments (HighRadius, Bill.com, Billtrust) that reduce WTP, and customers who won’t use portals. Notable role variations: senior finance roles (VP/Controller/CFO) show higher WTP and stronger interest in automation-driven ROI and security controls; AR/Collections and operational AP staff emphasize tailored remittance fields, reconciliation accuracy, and immediate pain relief (daily file, custom reports); very large enterprises often rate solution appeal but report low WTP when they’ve already invested in enterprise automation. Implication for personas: build two primary profiles — (A) Integration‑focused Finance Leaders (mid‑to‑large firms, DM power 2–4, WTP ~3–4) prioritizing security, ERPs and DSO reduction, and (B) Operations/AP/AR Executors (SMB to mid‑market, DM 1–3, WTP ~1–3) prioritizing fast go‑live, minimal IT lift, clear remittance data and low per‑user friction.
