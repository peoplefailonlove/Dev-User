Across 39 finance and ops respondents (Controllers, AR Managers, VPs/CFOs, Directors and Owners) common patterns are clear: buyers prioritize automation that reduces manual remittance work, improves cash application, and speeds collections. The most frequently selected features were a daily consolidated remittance file (delivered via email/SFTP), seamless “works with your automation & ERP” integration, Automated Delivery (scheduled payments + daily remittance), Custom Reports/Tailored Remittance, Check Conversion (convert checks to ACH/card), Bank Account Management and an Enterprise Portal with role-based controls. Preferred benefits are reduced manual processing, fewer reconciliation errors, faster DSO, and minimal IT lift / fast (weeks) implementation. Quantitatively, average willingness to pay across respondents is moderate (~2.7 on a 1–5 scale). Where explicit day measures were given, average DSO clusters near ~48 days; AR mixes are typically ACH/electronic‑heavy with most respondents reporting electronic dominance (many 60–95%), while paper checks persist (commonly ~5–9% of payments). Delivery preferences and integrations are consistent: daily consolidated remittance via email/SFTP and compatibility with existing cash application/ERP tools. Pain points repeated across industries: missing or unclear remittance data, time‑consuming reconciliation, reliance on paper checks, and customers’ reluctance to use portals. Respondents repeatedly requested minimal IT disruption and a single configurable remittance file rather than heavy new software. Behavioral traits include cost sensitivity and ROI scrutiny (many cite existing investments like HighRadius/Billtrust), cautious adoption when internal systems already exist, and strong operational focus—respondents want tangible time savings and DSO reduction. Notable role variations: AR managers and cash-application owners emphasize Check Conversion and remittance clarity for faster application and lower DSO; AP leaders and Controllers favor Enterprise Portal, bank-account routing and ERP integration to protect controls and simplify workflows; C-suite/owners and some private vendors value Dedicated Support/white‑glove onboarding and sometimes show higher WTP (several pvt vendors reported 4–5). Large enterprises that have invested in incumbent automation are less willing to pay or switch. These patterns should guide personas toward pragmatic, integration-first propositions emphasizing daily remittance, low IT lift, measurable DSO improvement, and segmented pricing/support for organizations with existing investments.
